A fraud modelthat knows whenit has gone stale.

Fraud labels arrive days late, so a deployed model can fail silently. Ours watches how its own SHAP explanations change on new, unlabelled transactions, and retrains only when its reasoning shifts.

The contribution

Explanation-drift retraining under delayed labels

We turn a shift in the model's own explanations into a decision about when to retrain, and add a label check for the shift explanations cannot see.

1

Measure explanation drift, without labels

For each chunk of new transactions, the share of SHAP attribution mass that moved away from recent training data: D = Σ W1(SHAPref, SHAPnow) / Σ E|SHAPref|. The alarm threshold is the 90th percentile of D on earlier training chunks.

2

Alarm, then adapt

The drifted chunk's own labels are not known yet, so retraining at once does not help. An alarm opens a 4-chunk window in which the model retrains each time new labels arrive.

3

Guard with delayed labels

When a chunk's labels arrive and the model caught significantly fewer than half of its frauds (one-sided binomial test, α = 0.05), that is also an alarm. It catches fraud that changes while the data looks normal.

0 vs 16

False alarms after a harmless shift

Fields the model ignores moved by 2 SD. Our monitor stayed quiet; the PSI input-drift monitor fired on all 16 later chunks and retrained 5 times for nothing.

5 vs 22

Retrains for the same recovery

After the harmful shift, 5 well-timed retrains reached PR-AUC 0.768, as good as retraining every chunk (0.766 with 22). Five retrains at random times reached only 0.745.

0.001 → 0.618

Recovery from a silent flip

When fraud flips sign, both label-free monitors are blind. The label guard notices once labels arrive and the model recovers with about 5 retrains.

What is borrowed and what is new. Explanation shift as a drift measure comes from Mougan et al. (2023). Our contribution is using it as a retraining policy for fraud under delayed labels, adding the alarm-then-adapt window and the binomial label guard, and testing it against a frozen model, periodic retraining, a PSI trigger and random retrain timing.

    The baseline study

    Why fraud is hard to learn

    Problem statement. Develop and evaluate a leakage-free ML framework for financial fraud detection that compares transaction-level models with temporal and behavioral models, and explains individual fraud predictions. The contribution above is built on this study.

    Class imbalance

    Only 0.172% of transactions are fraud. A model that says "legitimate" for everything scores 99.83% accuracy and catches zero fraud. We use PR-AUC, precision, recall and F1 instead.

    Temporal behavior

    Transactions arrive as a stream. Gap since the previous transaction, recent count and deviation from recent amounts carry signal that a single row does not.

    Evaluation leakage

    A random split trains on the future and tests on the past. A chronological split (train on early data, test on later) matches real deployment.

    Pipeline

    Raw transactions Audit and remove 1,081 duplicates Chronological split 60/20/20 Train-only preprocessing Static + past-only temporal features LR, RF, XGBoost, LightGBM, MLP LSTM / GRU over last k transactions Threshold tuned on validation SHAP explanations Explanation-drift monitor

    Leakage rules followed

    • Scaler and SMOTE are fit on training data only, inside pipelines.
    • Decision threshold and history window are chosen on validation data, never on test.
    • Temporal features use only transactions that happened before the current one.
    • The test period is evaluated once, after model selection is finished.

    Limits, ethics and next steps

    Limitations

    • Only 2 days of data; the real stream does not drift enough to trigger the monitor.
    • Shifts in the contribution are injected stress tests.
    • Label delay is simulated as one chunk (about 36 minutes); real chargebacks take days.
    • ULB features are anonymized PCA components with no customer IDs.
    • SHAP shows attribution, not causality.

    Ethics

    • False positives block real customers.
    • The model supports a human reviewer and does not decide alone.
    • Public anonymized data only.

    Next steps

    • Test the monitor on a dataset with real drift and card IDs (IEEE-CIS).
    • Model realistic label delays of days to weeks.
    • Temporal graph and transformer sequence models.
    • Cost-sensitive learning.

    Viva quick answers

    Where is the novelty?

    The models are the baseline. The contribution is a retraining policy: we watch how the model's SHAP explanations change on unlabelled transactions, retrain only when they shift, and add a delayed-label guard for what explanations cannot see. It raised no false alarms where PSI raised 16, and matched retraining every chunk with 5 retrains instead of 22.

    Isn't this just Mougan et al. (2023)?

    They proposed explanation shift as a detector. We use it as a retraining trigger for fraud under delayed labels, add the alarm-then-adapt window and the binomial label guard, and measure retrains saved and performance recovered against four baselines. We also show its blind spot (the sign flip).

    What does it improve on the real data?

    Nothing in PR-AUC, because the real 2-day stream does not drift: no monitor fires and the frozen model stays best (0.799). Blind periodic retraining spent 22 retrains and slightly lowered PR-AUC (0.793), while our monitor correctly held still.

    Why trust injected shifts?

    They are controlled experiments, like unit tests for a monitor: we know exactly when and what changed, so we can measure false alarms and detection delay. Each mimics a real failure: a logging change in unused fields, a unit change in key fields, and fraudsters changing behaviour.

    Why not use accuracy?

    With 0.172% fraud, predicting "legit" for everything gives 99.83% accuracy and catches nothing. PR-AUC, recall and precision focus on the rare fraud class.

    What is data leakage here?

    Any information from validation, test or the future reaching training: random splits that train on later transactions, fitting the scaler or SMOTE on all data, or choosing the threshold on the test set.

    Why apply SMOTE only after splitting?

    SMOTE makes synthetic frauds by interpolating between real ones. If done before the split, synthetic points built from test frauds leak into training and inflate results.

    Why is Random Forest best on test but XGBoost the final model?

    Models are selected on validation PR-AUC, where XGBoost was best. Choosing by test score would be selection on test, which is leakage.

    What is SHAP?

    SHapley Additive exPlanations: a game-theory attribution that splits a prediction into one contribution per feature; the contributions add up to the model output. For trees, TreeSHAP computes them exactly and fast.

    How did you use LSTM/GRU on ULB?

    Each sample is the window of the last k transactions (k = 5, 10, 20, 50) in time order, and the label is the last one. ULB has no card IDs, so the window is the global stream. At the window chosen on validation (k=5), GRU reached test PR-AUC 0.795 and LSTM 0.787, similar to tree models.

    What can your monitor not see?

    A change that only affects the rare fraud rows, such as the sign flip: the unlabelled data looks normal, so no label-free monitor can see it. That is why we added the delayed-label guard, which recovered PR-AUC from 0.001 to 0.618.
